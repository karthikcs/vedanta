In Vedantic philosophy (specifically detailed in texts like the Panchadashi), Priya, Moda, and Pramoda represent the three sequential stages or modes of mental joy (vṛttis) experienced through the Anandamaya Kosha (Bliss Sheath). They track how the mind experiences pleasure—from initial anticipation to acquisition, and finally to actual enjoyment.


## Priya (प्रिय) – The Joy of Anticipation

- *Definition*: The initial wave of pleasure experienced simply upon seeing or thinking of a desired object, person, or outcome.
- Example: Seeing your favorite meal listed on a menu or spotting a good friend walking toward you from a distance.

## Moda (मोद) – The Joy of Possession
- *Definition*: The heightened state of pleasure felt when you actually gain, receive, or acquire the desired object.
- *Example*: Having that favorite meal served at your table or receiving a long-awaited gift in your hands.

## Pramoda (प्रमोद) – The Joy of Enjoyment
- *Definition*: The maximum or intense joy derived from actually experiencing or consuming the object.
- *Example*: Eating and tasting the meal, or spending quality time conversing with that dear friend.

## The Vedantic Insight
According to Advaita Vedanta, the happiness felt during these three stages does not come from the external object itself. Instead:

- External objects merely temporarily quiet the mind's desires (vṛttis).

- When the mind becomes calm and quiet, the true, eternal bliss of the Atman (Inner Self) reflects clearly through the Sattva aspect of the Causal Body (Anandamaya Kosha).

- Once the enjoyment ends or a new desire arises, the reflection is obscured again.
